Mateusz Płoszaj-Mazurek

I study how daylight gets into buildings, and how the rules that are meant to guarantee it actually behave when you test them. Most of that work is computational: parametric studies, climate-based simulation, and fast models that predict daylight without running the simulation.

I trained as an architect, practised for ten years in Denmark and Poland, wrote a PhD on digital methods for building carbon footprint, and spent two years building design software for architects. Daylight is where those threads now meet.

Research

Daylight without the wait

OmegaFlux predicts useful daylight illuminance point by point from room geometry, using gradient-boosted trees on geometric view descriptors instead of a neural network. It runs in the browser, about two hundred times faster than the Radiance simulation it was trained on.

Repository · Live demo

Machine learning for low-carbon design

My PhD (2022) developed digital methods for estimating and reducing the carbon footprint of buildings while they are still being designed. The 2020 and 2024 articles in Energies carry that line: first parametric optimization with machine learning and a convolutional neural network, then language models and BIM data for building assessment tools.

Data for reuse and policy

I co-authored Poland's whole-life carbon roadmap for the built environment (2021), and published a workflow that uses a language model to enrich IFC data so large-panel building elements can be reused (2026).

OmegaFlux daylight prediction in the browser

Daylight prediction in milliseconds

Gradient-boosted trees on geometric view descriptors reach R² 0.986 on held-out rooms, about 200× faster than Radiance. On room shapes the model never saw, accuracy drops to R² 0.72: the honest limit of the method, and the subject of the next paper.

You describe the task; I reply with a fixed scope, a price and the method I'll use, before any work starts. Deliverables are files you own: the definition, the plugin, the script or the dataset, documented so your team can run it. Most engagements run between one week and two months.
